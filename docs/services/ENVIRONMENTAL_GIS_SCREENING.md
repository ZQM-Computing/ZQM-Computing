# Preliminary Environmental Constraints Mapping & Parcel GIS Screening

**Search intent:** preliminary environmental GIS screening; parcel constraints mapping; wetlands flood hazard GIS desktop assessment.

ZQM Computing offers proposed desktop GIS screening work to help planning and development teams organize available spatial indicators before commissioning field investigations or professional determinations. The output is an evidence-labeled mapping and data-quality product, **not a certified survey, environmental site assessment, delineation, engineering opinion, legal access finding or regulatory approval**.

## What can a desktop environmental GIS screen tell you?

A preliminary screen can intersect a client-authorized parcel or area of interest with appropriately licensed datasets, documenting source dates, resolution, scale, geometry uncertainty and spatial relationships. It can identify areas needing further investigation; it cannot establish site-specific conditions from public map geometry alone.

Possible layers, depending on location and rights, include published flood-hazard maps, wetlands inventory, elevation, land cover, protected areas, public transportation context and available parcel references. **Availability and interpretation vary by jurisdiction.** Never assume worldwide parcel coverage or uniform data accuracy.

## Recommended deliverable

1. **Input and jurisdiction:** client-authorized area or parcel identifier; agreed geography and objective.
2. **Source inventory:** dataset name, publisher, URL, publication/version date, license and coverage.
3. **QA checks:** CRS, geometry validity, units, positional accuracy, completeness and known temporal limitations.
4. **Preliminary overlay:** auditable intersection and proximity methods with explicit uncertainty and overlap handling.
5. **Findings packet:** annotated maps, source and limitations table, clearly differentiated observed indicators and interpretation, plus recommended verification steps.

## How should wetland or flood layers be interpreted?

A published wetland polygon may be useful as a screening indicator but does **not** replace jurisdictional determination or field delineation. Flood-hazard boundaries and elevation models also have documented limitations and changing versions. Any regulated decision should be reviewed using the authoritative current source and relevant qualified professionals.
